Five drops where the machine shows through

Today's set is unusually mechanical: a walk that is solved rather than keyframed, a list that only builds what the window can see, type that rolls on a rebuilt tape, a menu that the drag itself summons, and a slider whose transitions are shaders. Each one is rewritten from scratch so the technique is the only thing on screen.

DROP 01Animation

The Walk Is Solved, Not Keyframed

Three ink figures cross the page on a procedural gait: the planted foot slides back at travel speed, the swinging one follows a minimum-jerk path, and hip height falls out of what the standing leg can reach. Drag the hour and the whole palette turns to night.

DROP 02Layout

Write the Layout, Mount the Window

Masonry, timeline and board are three little functions that return a frame per item. Two thousand items exist; about twenty-five nodes are in the DOM. Switch windowing off and watch the counter — and the frame rate — tell you why.

DROP 03Typography

Everything Rolls, Not Just Digits

A reel is a one-glyph window over a tape that is rebuilt for every change, holding exactly the glyphs between the old value and the new one. So 7 → 2 really passes 8, 9, 0, 1 — and the same machine rolls letters and shapes.

DROP 04Micro-interaction

Drag First, Then Ask Where

Pick a file up and a wheel of actions fans out under the pointer. Which wedges appear depends on what you grabbed, the selection is pure angle maths because the pointer is captured, and resting on More turns the page without closing it.

DROP 05Component

The Slider Is a Shader

The slides are real elements in the document; a WebGL 2 canvas sits over them and draws the gap between two of them. One exactly-solved spring takes drag, flick, wheel and arrow keys — and when it settles, the loop stops drawing entirely.
