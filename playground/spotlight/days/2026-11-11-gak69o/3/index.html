A typewriter with no script

The glyphs are a ::before whose width is animated in whole ch steps; the caret is an ::after that blinks on its own track. Everything you see below is four keyframe rules — the sliders only rewrite custom properties.

one string · type, hold, delete
Hello, operator.

The keyframe percentages are the whole program: 0–30% types, 30–62% holds, 62–86% deletes. steps(--n) makes the width land on character boundaries, which is why it never shows half a glyph.

three strings · one slot
no runtime, no bundle at all, just keyframes

Three strings share one grid cell. An opacity track gives each a third of the cycle, and each ::before runs at a third of the duration, delayed by --slot — so a string is always at zero width the moment it takes the stage. Colour, weight and tracking stay with the string that declared them.

a hesitation, written in
Stand by… cleared for orbit.

A flat stretch in the middle of the keyframes costs exactly what typing a few more characters would, so the line can stall on the ellipsis and then finish. Screen readers get the finished sentence from a visually hidden copy, never the half-typed one.
