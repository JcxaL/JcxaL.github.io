Five techniques that hand the arithmetic back to the browser

Today's drops all replace something we used to compute by hand. A sprite morph becomes a generated @keyframes rule instead of a canvas loop. Flex lines balance themselves instead of waiting for a breakpoint. The half-leading above a cap height is trimmed by the text box, not a negative margin. A hover shear is a stack of transitions rather than a tween. And a toast resizes on a spring solver instead of a duration.

1animation

Every pixel is paired, then tweened

Two pixel sprites, one box-shadow. Each lit pixel of the first is matched to a pixel of the second, the pair is interpolated over a handful of frames, and the whole flight is written out as a keyframes rule — nothing runs in JS at playback.

Wrapping that refuses to strand one item

flex-wrap: balance keeps the line count a greedy wrap would produce and spreads the items evenly across it, and flex-line-count sets a floor. Drag the width and watch the orphan row disappear — with the same rule polyfilled where the value is missing.

One picture, cut into parts that lag

The image is never transformed. It is sliced into bands carrying the same background at different offsets, and on hover each band slides its own distance after its own delay. Four shear patterns, all of it plain CSS transitions.

One toast that morphs in place

Not a stack. The next call reaches into the toast already on screen and changes its icon, text, height and progress, and the box springs to its new size. Interrupt a move mid-flight and the velocity carries over instead of resetting.
