Rhythm

Each column computes --gap: calc(1lh - 1ex) from its own font. Three typefaces measure themselves; one stylesheet.

Spacing that comes from the font

Design systems pick 8px and hope. But the only gap a reader actually sees is the one between the bottom of one line and the top of the next — baseline-to-baseline minus x-height. CSS can measure that now: calc(1lh - 1ex). Change the typeface and every margin on the page re-measures itself.

This browser does not support text-box: trim-both, so the specimens keep their half-leading. The 1lh − 1ex spacing still works.

A tall x-height

This face packs a lot of letter into its line, so the visible channel between lines is narrow — and every margin derived from it tightens to match.

  • Headings, paragraphs and lists
  • all share one derived unit

Nothing here is a magic number. Change the family and watch the whole column re-space itself.

ui-serifgap —

A smaller x-height

The same line-height leaves more open channel here, because the letters occupy less of it. The gap grows, and the rhythm stays even to the eye rather than even on a ruler.

  • Same markup
  • Same stylesheet

An 8px module would have made this column look cramped next to its neighbour.

ui-monospacegap —

A third metric

Monospace faces sit differently again. One rule, three results — which is the point.

  • --gap: calc(1lh - 1ex)
  • margin: var(--gap)

Drag line-height in the panel: the bands move together, never apart.

Turn on the gap bands and drag line height. Each column keeps the band size its own typeface asks for, and every margin in it is a multiple of that.
