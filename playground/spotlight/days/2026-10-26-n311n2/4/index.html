Drop #4 · Micro-interaction

Brighter Than White

An HDR display has headroom above page white. dynamic-range-limit decides how much of it an element may spend — so a hover can light a badge past the brightest pixel around it.

Hover a badge — it claims the headroom

Each badge sits at dynamic-range-limit: standard until you point at it, then switches to the limit chosen in the panel. On an SDR screen the brightness and bloom fallbacks still fire, so the gesture reads everywhere — the extra stop of light is the bonus, not the whole effect.

a mark lit past paper white — the trick behind SoVeryBright's HDR logo exports

Gain-map images carry an SDR picture plus the instructions to brighten it, so one <img> serves both kinds of screen. CSS reaches the same headroom with HDR colour spaces such as color(rec2100-hlg …), and dynamic-range-limit keeps thumbnails from shouting while a hero image glows.
