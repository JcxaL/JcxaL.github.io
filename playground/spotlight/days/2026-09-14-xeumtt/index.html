Skylines, clipping & underlines that move

Five patterns pulled from this fortnight's design web, each stripped back to the mechanism and rebuilt in vanilla HTML, CSS and JavaScript — no frameworks, no CDN. Words extruded into skylines, the overflow value that quietly breaks your layout, a native underline you can animate, tooltips that wait before they speak, and an accordion the browser can search while it is still folded.

Type as Architecture

A word is sampled into a grid and every filled cell extrudes into a tower that rises on a staggered timeline, then keeps orbiting. Three skylines, one 2D canvas each, no 3D library — the stagger is what makes it read as construction.

Clip Is Not Hidden

They look identical and they are not. overflow: hidden quietly makes a box a scroll container — scriptable, and fatal to position: sticky inside it. clip just clips, and brings overflow-clip-margin so a hover lift can escape.

Underline Choreography

The browser's own underline is already movable: text-underline-offset is a length that animates, so the line travels instead of fading. Chrome 154 adds text-decoration-inset and text-decoration-skip-spaces on top, switched on wherever the engine has them.

Hover Intent

A tooltip that opens the instant you cross it is noise; one that always makes you wait is sluggish. Two timers and a shared warm flag: wait for the first open, then let every neighbour open instantly, with a grace window on the way out.

Inspired by Abhishek Jakhar · blog.master.dev

Findable When Folded

Every accordion you have shipped hides its answers from ⌘F. Content folded with hidden="until-found" stays searchable — the browser finds it, fires beforematch, and unfolds the panel itself.
