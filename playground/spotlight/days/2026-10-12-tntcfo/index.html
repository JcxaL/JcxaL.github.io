Five ways the browser measures things for you

Today's drops share a thread: each one hands a measurement back to the engine instead of doing it in JavaScript. Scaling that layout believes in, a box that hugs its own wrapped text, type sized to the space it has, a border painted straight from a background, and a checkbox that never stopped being one.

01animation

Zoom That Reflows

Chrome 150 made zoom animatable — and zoom is not transform: scale(). Three side-by-side tracks show the difference: zoom displaces its neighbours and re-wraps its text, a transform just magnifies.

02layout

Hug the Wrap

Roman Komarov's shrinkwrap: an anchored probe exposes a view timeline, two registered properties read its edges as numbers, and their difference becomes the width. CSS used as a ruler — no JavaScript measures anything.

03typography

Type Measured to the Box

Headlines that fill whatever room is left. text-fit asks the layout engine for the size instead of stacking clamp() on container units — with a binary-search fallback so you can see it either way.

Borders From the Background

No doubled pseudo-element, no masked wrapper. background-clip: border-area clips one ordinary background to the border box, so a conic gradient becomes the border and the corners keep their curve.

A Checkbox Worth Keeping

Andy Bell's stopgap until ::checkmark exists: the real input stays, stretched invisibly over the box it controls, so focus, keyboard and form submission survive. Everything else scales from one font-size.
