Boxes that hug wrapped text

A block with wrapping text fills its whole container, even when the longest line stops far short of the edge — which is why chat bubbles and tooltips end up with dead space. Roman Komarov's shrinkwrap technique fixes it by using CSS as a measuring instrument: an anchored probe exposes a view timeline, two registered custom properties read its edges as numbers, and the difference becomes the element's width.

1 · chat bubbles

The classic case. Each bubble wraps at the same limit but ends up only as wide as its longest line.

2 · right-aligned, where the gap shows most

With the content aligned to the end, an unhugged box leaves its ragged edge floating away from the rail.

3 · a headline, measured live

Drag max inline-size and watch the measured width track the longest line rather than the limit.

The two numbers the browser animates are --sw-start and --sw-end: fractions of a 10 000px resolution range. Their difference, multiplied back out, is the content's real width — no JavaScript measured anything on this page.
