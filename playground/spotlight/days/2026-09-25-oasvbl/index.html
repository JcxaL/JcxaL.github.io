Marks, Probes, Graphemes, Ranges & Moods

Today's five are all about reaching something the browser normally keeps to itself. A seeded function replaces the illustrator's hand. An anchored probe answers a question CSS has no query for. A segmenter tells you where a character really ends. A range reaches inside a form field. And six numbers replace a sprite sheet full of faces.

DROP 01Animation

Every Mark Is a Function

Every stroke is generated from a seed: the wobble, the overshoot past the corner, the second pass that never quite retraces the first. Same seed, same drawing on every machine — bump it eight times a second and you get the boil of hand-drawn animation.

DROP 02Layout

Overlap, Detected

Anchor positioning stretches an invisible strip between two elements and parks a fixed-width child in it. When the gap closes the strip overflows, its scroll timeline goes live, and a style query lets the decoration get out of the way.

DROP 03Typography

Split It Properly

Per-letter animation starts by splitting the string, and [...text] tears a family emoji into seven pieces, strands Devanagari vowel signs and detaches accents. Intl.Segmenter splits by grapheme, by word where there are no spaces, and by sentence.

DROP 04Micro-interaction

Inside the Input

You can read an input's value but never ask where its third word sits on screen. Edge's new OpaqueRange hands you live ranges inside form fields — here it anchors a mention popover, squiggles one bad substring and opens a picker at the caret, with a mirror shim where it hasn't shipped.

DROP 05Component

One Ball, Thirty Moods

The status spinner is becoming a face. No sprite sheet: an expression is six numbers — lid opening, eye curve, brow angle, mouth bend, mouth opening, body squash — and changing mood just springs them somewhere else. Still legible at 26px.
