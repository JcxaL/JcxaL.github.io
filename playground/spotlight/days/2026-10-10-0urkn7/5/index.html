Tune the ticks

The slider's ticks are rebuilt by editing the <datalist> only. The input is never touched.

The list the browser
already draws

<datalist> is the oldest trick here and the least used: one element, linked by list, and four different controls grow a suggestion UI you would otherwise build by hand. Tick marks on a slider. A filtering dropdown on a text field. Preset swatches in the colour picker. No library, no listener, no portal.

Tick marks on a range

input[type=range][list]

Every <option> value becomes a notch under the track. Drag the panel's tick-count slider and watch the scale redraw; switch the list attribute off and they all vanish.

Ticks don't have to be even

non-uniform scale

The options are just values, so a logarithmic or editorial scale notches exactly where it should — no background-image gradient hack, no absolutely positioned <span>s.

A filtering combobox

input[list] + free text

Start typing — the browser filters the list, keyboards get it for free, and the field still accepts a value that isn't on the list. That last part is what <select> can never do.

Swatches and suggested dates

type=color · type=date

Open the colour picker: the datalist seeds it with your brand presets. The date field offers its options the same way. One mechanism, four very different native UIs.
