Spring & flick

last gesture: —

A switch you can
flick

Most web switches are a checkbox with a transition: you click, it slides, the end. A switch that feels physical has to accept the other two gestures as well — a drag that tracks your finger exactly, and a flick that commits on velocity rather than distance. All three below are one component: a real role="switch" button with a pointer track and a spring bolted on.

1 · Three switches, one behaviour

Click them, drag the thumb slowly, or flick and let go halfway. A slow drag past the commit point wins on position; a fast flick wins on velocity even from a standing start.

Still a button. Tab to one and press Space or Enter: the keyboard path never touches the drag code, and aria-checked is the single source of truth that both paths write to.

2 · What the spring is doing

Live trace of the top switch: position in amber, velocity in blue. Drag it around and the two curves separate — that gap is the whole reason a flick can commit from below the halfway line.
