::target-text

Pick a style, then fire any chip on the left. The pseudo-element styles whatever the #:~:text= directive matched — text nobody gave an id to.

Link to the sentence, not the page

A text fragment — #:~:text=start,end — scrolls to a phrase the author never wrapped in an anchor, and the browser highlights what it found. ::target-text is the hook that lets you style that highlight, so a deep link arrives looking like your product instead of a system selection.

What a fragment can point at

Sample article · Day 134 · scroll target demo

The fragment identifier used to have exactly one job: find the element with this id. A text directive splits that into three unrelated jobs — find a string, scroll it into view, and mark it — none of which need the document to have planned for you.

Why it matters for product links

Support replies, changelog notes and AI answers all want to cite one sentence in a long document. Before text fragments the only honest options were to link the whole page and say “see the third paragraph”, or to ask an engineer to add an id. A text directive takes the quote itself as the address, which means the citation keeps working on a page that never shipped an anchor for it.

A range, not just a word

A range directive takes a start and an end, separated by a comma, and matches everything between them — useful when the quote is longer than a phrase but you would rather not paste three hundred characters into a URL. The browser scrolls to the start and highlights the range it matched.

Prefix and suffix, for ambiguity

When a phrase appears more than once, prefix-, and ,-suffix pin down which occurrence you meant without putting either into the highlight. The dash is part of the syntax, which is why it looks so strange the first time you read one.

What it refuses to do

It will not match across an element boundary it considers a block break, it is restricted to user-initiated navigations so a page cannot quietly steal your scroll position, and a directive that finds nothing simply does nothing. Browsers also strip the directive out of location.hash, so script cannot read what was highlighted — which is why this demo prints the directive it built rather than the one the browser kept, and why a long one can look quietly truncated in the address bar.
