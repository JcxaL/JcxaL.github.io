Trails, sizes, clamps,
corners & parameters

Today's five are all about saying less to mean the same thing. Four of them are declarations that collapse something you used to spell out by hand — two axes, three clamp rules, two corners, a whole themed asset — and the fifth is a scroll trail built the way motion work is actually built: animate numbers, let a renderer read them.

01ANIMATION

Ghost Trails

Letters that remember where they have been. Each glyph keeps a ring buffer of its own past placements and strokes them back out as outlined ghosts, so the trail follows the real motion instead of faking it with offset copies. Scroll drives one number; the renderer does the rest.

One Declaration, Both Axes

size, min-size and max-size landed in Safari Technology Preview 254. One value squares a box, two read inline-then-block — and the four physical min/max properties that quietly break in vertical writing mode collapse into two logical ones.

Clamping, Taken Apart

-webkit-line-clamp was one knob with a forced flexbox attached. CSS Overflow 4 splits it into continue, max-lines and block-ellipsis — so you can finally clamp by height, or end the cut with your own string instead of a fixed ellipsis.

Two Corners at a Time

border-top-radius and its three siblings own two corners each. The win is in motion: a side shorthand transitions only its own pair, so the corners you did not touch stop joining the animation — and stop snapping to zero when you forget a value.
