Two corners at a time

Every time a component needed to round one edge and square the other, we wrote the four-value border-radius and then spent a minute working out which value was which corner. CSS Borders 4 adds side shorthands — border-top-radius, border-bottom-radius, border-left-radius, border-right-radius — and suddenly the declaration says what the designer said.

your browser has the side shorthandsno side shorthands here — four-value border-radius is standing in

1 · A TAB THAT JOINS ITS PANEL — CLICK

Only the top two corners animate. The bottom edge stays square so the tab and the panel read as one surface — border-top-radius alone, with border-bottom-radius: 0 never touched again.

2 · A SHEET THAT ROUNDS AS IT LIFTS — HOVER OR FOCUS

DOCKED → LIFTED

Docked, the sheet is flush with the frame and its top corners are square. Lifted, it rounds — one property transitioning, not four values racing each other.

3 · SQUISH ON PRESS — HOLD EACH BUTTON

The quiet win is the transition. Animating one side used to mean animating the whole four-value shorthand, so the corners you did not want to touch were interpolating too — and any value you forgot to repeat snapped to 0 mid-transition. A side shorthand only ever owns its two corners, so the other two are not part of the animation at all.
