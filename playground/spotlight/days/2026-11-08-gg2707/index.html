Five effects driven by a measurement, not a timeline

None of today's drops animate on a duration. A luminance value picks a glyph. A corner spec becomes a path. A scroll delta decides how badly a word breaks. An amplitude envelope sets a radius. A getComputedStyle read writes the redline. Change the input and the output follows — there is no keyframe in the middle holding a second opinion.

1animation

Scenes that render into a <pre>

A z-buffered torus, an additive plasma and a height-field sea — none of them drawn. Each frame is sampled to a luminance grid and every cell picks a character from a brightness ramp, so the whole animation is text. Swap the ramp for blocks or katakana and the geometry is untouched.

2layout

A bevel here, a scoop there, one path

Per-vertex corners — bevel, notch, scoop, squircle — generated as a single clip-path: shape() and painted twice: full size in the stroke colour, inset by the stroke width in the surface colour. That second pass is what gives a shaped box a real border. corner-shape sits alongside for comparison.

3typography

Headlines that shred at speed

Scroll distance per frame is smoothed into an energy value, and each character rolls against it — outer letters weighted to break first. Stop scrolling and the glyphs resolve one at a time, because the energy decays rather than snapping back. No stagger lists, no keyframes.

4micro-interaction

One point cloud, four states

Resting, listening, thinking, answering — all from the same few hundred particles. Radius tracks an envelope, a bright band sweeps the latitudes while it works, and syllable onsets eject particles outward so output never looks like input. Tap an orb to drive it by hand.

5component

A component taken apart, honestly

Six paint layers, each a clone of the live card with the other layers' paint made transparent. The measurements are read off the real element, states are forced through the component's own declarations, and pressing T swaps raw pixels for the token names the author actually wrote.
