Anchored, docked, flowed

Before CSS had anything to say about it, resizable windows solved layout with three rules computed in code: every control holds anchors to the edges it must follow, or docks to one side and eats the space, or joins a flow that wraps. Drag the hatched corner of any window and watch the rule — not the stylesheet — decide what moves.

click a control

Anchor none and the control keeps its proportional place. Anchor both sides and it stretches. Dock and it claims a whole edge of whatever space is left.

The whole system is one pass over the children. Docked controls are carved out of the client rectangle in order, each shrinking what remains; anchored controls then get Δleft = Δw·(a.left ? 0 : a.right ? 1 : ½) and Δwidth = Δw·(a.left&&a.right ? 1 : 0), vertically the same. Flow just walks the leftovers and wraps.
